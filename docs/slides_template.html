Game 3: bargain for GPUs and discover backward induction

Split 10,000 stolen GPUs→You offer (slider)→AI accepts or rejects→He offers, you decide→×2 (4 rounds)→Backward-induction challenge→Game-tree debrief
{{IMG:d3_04_result_card}}
You offer a split of 10,000 stolen GPUs; the AI accepts or rejects. A rejection means nobody gets anything.
{{IMG:d3_07_debrief_game_tree}}
Theory says offer 500 → (9,500, 500). Then the debrief compares that with what you two actually did.
The AI really decides: it rejected a 1,000 offer ("1,000 is an insult!") and remembered it two rounds later.

Three AI characters talk; code referees every number

{{SVG:architecture}}
  • Detective Zhou: interrogates and announces verdicts
  • Tung Tung Tung Sahur: picks moves; in Game 3 he really decides to accept or reject
  • Nash-sensei: hints, challenges, and debriefs, without giving answers away
  • No AI boss: a Python orchestrator runs turns; agents get read-only tools and typed, validated outputs
